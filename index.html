How we travel

Light first, crowds never

Two photographers with taste, not tourists. Every day is built around one great shot at either end of the day, somewhere to sleep that's worth filming, good food without the fuss, and one thing that's just fun.

  • Be on the trail before the coaches and stay out after they leave. Golden hour runs about 8 to 9pm in February.
  • Stays are design-led, quiet and always have two bedrooms or two separate beds. No chains, no backpacker energy.
  • Nothing cheesy, no bungy. Motorsport, a jet boat into a national park, a lake sauna, padel, cedar hot pools.
  • Fiordland gets the flexible days. If the forecast turns, the Milford Road day swaps with a Te Anau day.

    The alternative

    Luxury RV

    A motorhome puts you on the lake edge for first light without a check-in time in sight. It's the better camera, the worse bedroom.

    • The van: a maui Beach (7.55 m) or Cascade (6.74 m). Both are 4 berths with two separate double beds, so you get one each. Skip the 2 berth Ultima; it's one shared double. maui Elite guarantees a van under a year old for about NZ$55 a day more.
    • Pick up: maui and Britz share a depot at 9 Purple Ash Ave, Frankton, about 3 km from the airport. It closes at 4:30pm and you land at 4:40pm, so night one is a stay and you collect the van at 8am Tuesday. Drop it back by early afternoon on the 25th.
    • Cost: maui doesn't publish New Zealand rates. Wilderness quotes NZ$650 to $750 a day for February, so budget about NZ$6,000 to $7,000 for nine days before insurance, fuel and sites. That's roughly the same as the stays. Wilderness itself only operates from Auckland and Christchurch, so it doesn't suit a Queenstown loop.
    • Rules: since 7 June 2026 a vehicle needs a green self-containment warrant, with a fixed toilet, to use self-contained-only sites. Rental vans should already comply; check the card is in the window.
    • The sites: Kinloch DOC campsite at the head of Lake Wakatipu (book ahead), Glendhu Bay on Lake Wānaka (book early), The Pines on the Lake Pukaki shore (free, self-contained only, no bookings), and Cascade Creek DOC campsite on the Milford Road (must be booked online), which puts you an hour from Milford before the buses.
    • The smart version: stays everywhere except Pukaki and the Milford Road, where sleeping on the water is the whole point. That's two nights in a van, or just the right stays.

      For the cameras

      Shooting notes

      • Full moon mid trip. It's full around 20 to 21 Feb (there's a penumbral lunar eclipse on the 20th), which is your Mount Cook stretch. That rules out Milky Way shots in the Dark Sky Reserve but makes moonlit glaciers and Aoraki very doable. Plan night shoots around moonrise rather than darkness.
      • Light: sunrise moves from about 7:01am to 7:15am and sunset from about 8:56pm to 8:40pm over the trip. Sun rises east south east and sets west south west, so sunset lights Aoraki and the Pukaki side head on. Fiordland valleys lose direct sun early.
      • Drones need a DOC permit on any conservation land or water, including all three national parks on the route. Green zones are approved online for NZ$180 plus GST and last a year; orange zones start at NZ$2,450 plus GST and are assessed case by case. Check the zone map now and apply early.
      • Airspace: the Queenstown control zone covers the whole Whakatipu Basin, so no casual flying around town or the Crown Range lookout. Stay under 120 m, in line of sight, and at least 4 km from aerodromes (Wānaka, Glenorchy and the Pukaki strip all count). Queenstown Lakes council isn't approving flights over its reserves without a Part 102 certificate.
      • The cleanest legal drone spots are private land with the owner's say so, so ask your hosts. A trail easement over farmland (Isthmus Peak) gives walking access only, not drone permission.

        Worth knowing

        • Anything marked "about" or "check" came from a reseller or an undated page, not the operator's current price. Confirm before you book.
        • NZ's first Michelin Guide landed in July 2026, so the starred tables (Rata, Sherwood, Kika, Essence) will be harder to get than usual.
